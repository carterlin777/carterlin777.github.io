Li'l Uzi Vert(ical)

Compact Vertical Spinner Designed for Impact Stability

Design Objective

The robot was designed under strict weight and size constraints with the goal of maintaining control during impacts. Instead of maximizing size or reach, the design prioritizes stability and rapid re-engagement.

Core Design Strategy

The primary design decision was to minimize overall footprint and concentrate mass toward the center of the robot. This reduces displacement during impacts and allows the robot to maintain contact and control following collisions.

Mechanical Design

The master sketch defined all major constraints including footprint and component placement. Parts were designed for tight packaging and structural integrity under repeated impacts.

Weapon Design

The vertical spinner uses a brushless motor to achieve high rotational speeds. Tip speed was estimated using motor KV and battery voltage:

Tip Speed ≈ π × Diameter × RPM

With a 3.2 inch weapon diameter and a 2300 KV motor at 11.1V, the theoretical maximum tip speed is approximately 240 mph. Actual speed is lower due to load and system losses, but this provided a basis for comparing design tradeoffs.

A smaller diameter was chosen to allow faster spin-up and better control, sacrificing some reach and stored energy for responsiveness.

Electronics System

The system separates drive and weapon control to balance simplicity and performance.

  • Battery: 300mAh HV LiPo (high voltage for power density)
  • Drive: Dual brushed ESC (reliable, simple control)
  • Weapon: Brushless motor + ESC (high RPM capability)
  • Receiver: Flysky FS2A (compact and compatible)
  • Switch: Fingertech power switch for safe operation

Design focused on compact wiring and minimizing interference with the weapon system.

Performance

In competition, the compact design reduced knockback and allowed the robot to remain grounded during impacts. This enabled immediate re-engagement and control, leading to a decisive win.

Lessons Learned

While compact packaging improved stability, it reduced accessibility for maintenance and repair. Future iterations would improve serviceability while maintaining centralized mass distribution.
